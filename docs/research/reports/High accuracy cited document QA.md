# Fix the Ruler Before Chasing Ninety-Five

No peer-reviewed document-QA system reports 95-99% answer accuracy on the kinds of documents Vault serves. The best verified result is **92.0% on FinanceBench** (GPT-5-mini; Claude Sonnet 4.5 scores 91.78%). It comes from an agent that searches, greps and opens line-numbered windows inside one 117k-token filing, scores within 2 points of an oracle given the gold page (94.0%), and spends **7.8x the tokens** of single-shot RAG, which scored 24.24% in the same paper ([AgenticRAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05538v1)). On contracts and scientific papers, no reviewed system reaches 90%. CUAD snippet recall@8 is 40.7% with a strong embedder ([LegalBench-RAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.10343v1)). The best open system for papers, PaperQA2, reaches **85.2% precision but only 66.0% accuracy** ([PaperQA2](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2409.13740)). "95-99%" is therefore credible for Vault in three narrow senses. Citation integrity is already 1.00, by construction. Claim faithfulness to cited text can be pushed toward the mid-90s with a verify-and-repair loop. Selective accuracy (accuracy on the questions Vault chooses to answer) can be pushed high with calibrated abstention. Raw answer accuracy on long 10-Ks can plausibly reach the 80s-90s with in-document navigation. Vault also cannot currently measure any of these at that level. Its 44-item train split has a ±14-point interval, its gold is draft, and its completeness judge was wrong on at least 2 of 5 audited failures. Phase 1 must therefore be an evaluation rebuild. After it, the system changes are ranked by how many of the 16 observed failures each removes per dollar and per millisecond: structure-aware table and section indexing, contextual index text, entity-quota retrieval with a coverage check, a code-owned verify-and-repair pass, a scored abstention combiner, latency overlap, and finally a routed agentic "locate" loop for long filings.

Confidence tags used throughout: **VERIFIED** means read in the primary source by the research team. **CLAIMED** means secondary, abstract-level or search-summary evidence. **VENDOR** means published by a party selling the technique. **INFERENCE** means our own derivation, and every computed number comes with the command that regenerates it.

## Ninety-five percent is a property of the harness, not the retriever

### Realistic ceilings differ by document type

The literature splits cleanly by document type, and the split decides what Vault should promise.

FinanceBench is the only benchmark where a reported system clears 90%, and the architecture matters more than the model. In the original setups, GPT-4-Turbo with a shared vector store scored about **19%**, while the same model with the full filing in context scored about **76-79%** (CLAIMED, secondary sources disagree on the exact number) ([Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/financebench)). "Enhanced" RAG with query rewriting, filename prepending and finance-adapted embeddings reached **47-55%** (CLAIMED) ([arXiv 2411.07142](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.07142)). One vendor reports Claude Opus 4.6 at 87.3% through its agentic endpoint (VENDOR, figure from a search snippet) ([Dewey](https://meetdewey.com/blog/financebench-eval)). The 92% result is VERIFIED but was scored by an LLM judge plus manual review, and the paper does not describe its traditional-RAG baseline ([AgenticRAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05538v1)). FinanceBench questions name the company and filing, so "which document" is nearly solved. The hard part is locating one table line inside roughly 143 pages, and that is exactly where Vault's 10-K misses sit.

Contracts are a retrieval-recall problem. LegalBench-RAG scores character-level snippet recall over CUAD. With naive chunking, no reranker and text-embedding-3-large, recall is **12.6 at k=1, 40.7 at k=8 and 75.7 at k=64**, and the Cohere reranker made results *worse* (VERIFIED, with a duplicated-table anomaly in the paper) ([LegalBench-RAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.10343v1)). No paper reports end-to-end CUAD answer accuracy near 90%.

Scientific papers are a metric-comparability problem. QASPER answer F1 ranges from about 22 to 55 depending on protocol, and the numbers are not comparable across papers. Free-form F1 stays low even when answers are correct (CLAIMED) ([Eugene Yan](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/qa-evals/); [LongRAG](https://arxiv.org/html/arXiv:2406.15319)). DISRetrieval's key fact is more useful for Vault than any leaderboard number. Given golden evidence of about 129 words, a reader scores **50.71 F1**, against 48.81 with the full 4,170-word paper and about 39-41 with top-5 retrieval. **Retrieval is the bottleneck** (VERIFIED) ([DISRetrieval](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06313v4)).

| Document type | Best verified or claimed result | Method that produced it | Realistic Vault target after this plan (INFERENCE) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10-K / FinanceBench | 92.0% accuracy; oracle 94.0% (VERIFIED) | Agentic in-document search/find/open | 80-90% with a routed locate loop; about 65-75% with index fixes alone |
| Contracts / CUAD | Recall@8 40.7 (character-level), no answer-accuracy SOTA (VERIFIED) | Retrieval benchmark only | High precision with abstention; recall bounded by multi-span clauses. Report span recall, not one accuracy number |
| Papers / QASPER | Gold-evidence ceiling 50.71 F1 for a 3B reader; PaperQA2 66% accuracy at 85.2% precision on LitQA2 | Sentence-level retrieval plus deep LLM re-scoring | Judge-scored pass rate in the 80s on answerable items is plausible. Do not quote 90%+ |
| Multi-document compare/aggregate | MEBench: "advanced models achieve only 59%" (CLAIMED) | none reaches high accuracy | Enforce coverage in code; report the per-side coverage rate |
| Citation integrity | 1.00 by construction | Handles copied by code | Already met; keep it as an invariant test |

The FinanceBench row also exposes an open question in Vault's own data. Vault's whole-document route showed "no detectable gain on single-document items at 2x cost". At n=44 the paired minimum detectable effect is about **13-19 points** (INFERENCE; see the sizing table below), so that result cannot rule out a 10-point gain. The literature's large long-context gains are also on 100k+ token filings, which may not be the items Vault tested. Re-run that comparison on a FinanceBench-heavy stratum after Phase 1, not before.

### The headline number must be decomposed before it can be raised

"Accuracy and faithfulness" covers four quantities that fail for different reasons. Correctness is whether the claims match gold. Completeness is recall over gold's required claims. Citation quality is whether each claim's cited sentences entail it, and whether each citation is needed. Abstention quality is the false-refusal rate on answerable items against the correct-refusal rate on unanswerable ones. CRAG shows why the last one matters. It scores a correct answer +1, an acceptable one 0.5, "I don't know" 0 and a wrong answer **-1**, and its best industry system reached **62.6% perfect with 17.9% hallucination** (VERIFIED) ([CRAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04744)). A system that claims 95% but hallucinates on every unanswerable question is worse than one at 85% that abstains correctly. Vault should report **truthfulness = accuracy − hallucination rate** alongside answer_pass. That gives the reader one number that cannot be gamed by answering everything.

## Phase 1: rebuild the evaluation before touching the pipeline

The case for doing evaluation first is arithmetic. At answer_pass 0.64 on 44 items, the 95% half-width is **±14.2 points**. At 122 items it is ±8.5, and at p=0.9 on 122 items it is ±5.3 (INFERENCE; regenerate with `python3 -c "import math;[print(n,round(196*math.sqrt(p*(1-p)/n),1)) for n in (44,122,300,600) for p in (.64,.9)]"`). Several of the changes below have literature effects of 3-10 points. Without a bigger, cleaner set, Vault cannot tell any of them from noise. The second reason is label noise. Vendrow et al. re-labeled 15 popular benchmarks and found that "for the majority of benchmarks we investigate, more than half of model failures can be attributed to label noise" (CLAIMED) ([Vendrow et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03461v1)). In the GSM8K-Platinum cleanup, about half of the model-flagged items (**110 of 219**) were dropped as ambiguous or ill-posed ([GSM8K-Platinum](https://huggingface.co/datasets/madrylab/gsm8k-platinum/blob/main/README.md)). Vault's finding that at least 2 of 5 sampled "incomplete" failures were judge errors fits this pattern. At a 95% target, gold and judge errors dominate the residual, so they have to be removed first.

### Evaluation and golden dataset design: stratify on an explicit edge-case taxonomy

**Best-evidenced strategy.** Stratify the gold set on an explicit question-type taxonomy, plant strong negatives, and store gold as atomic required claims. CRAG's mix is Simple 27%, Simple-with-condition 16%, Set 9%, Comparison 12%, Aggregation 11%, Multi-hop 9%, Post-processing 4% and **False premise 12%** (VERIFIED) ([CRAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04744)). ARES generates strong negatives from same-document passages, BM25-retrieved passages and contradictory answers. It keeps a synthetic question only if the retriever ranks its source passage first, a round-trip filter against trivially easy items (VERIFIED) ([ARES](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09476v2)).

**For Vault.** Most of the expansion is sampling and auditing, not authoring. FinanceBench's public 150 questions, QASPER's evidence-annotated questions and LegalBench-RAG's CUAD spans already exist with gold evidence. Hand-authoring is needed only for the strata no public set covers: unanswerable, false premise, near-duplicate document versions, cross-document contradiction, and prompt injection inside document text. Store gold as required claims, optional claims and acceptable alternates (numeric formats, units, synonyms), with gold evidence as sentence-handle ranges. The judge then checks entailment claim by claim instead of comparing prose. Cap questions per source document to limit clustering (see below). Paraphrase authored questions away from the source wording, because questions written from a chunk overlap lexically with that chunk and inflate recall (INFERENCE).

**Step.** Build `vault_eval_v2` with the strata below, frozen into train / dev / test splits by document, so that no document spans two splits. Target sizes are INFERENCE, chosen so each decision-grade stratum reaches a half-width of about ±10 points and the pooled test set about ±4.

| Stratum | Source | Target n (all splits) | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-span factual | QASPER, CUAD, FinanceBench | 120 | Base rate |
| 10-K table lookup and numeric (incl. calculation) | FinanceBench | 100 | Half of observed retrieval misses |
| Paper multi-hop and "which dataset/baseline" | QASPER | 80 | The other half of the misses |
| Multi-span / clause list | CUAD | 60 | Recall-limited answers |
| Compare / conflict across documents | Authored over the corpus | 60 | The observed missing-side failures |
| Unanswerable (absent) | Authored | 50 | Measures false answers |
| False premise | Authored | 30 | CRAG's 12% category |
| Near-duplicate or versioned document distractor | Authored (two versions of one contract) | 25 | ARES-style strong negative |
| Prompt injection in document text | Authored | 15 | Safety invariant, gated as must-not-regress |
| Formatting long tail (footnotes, multi-column, scanned page) | Sampled | 20 | Extraction regressions |

That totals about **560 items**, with test at about 40% (about 225). The existing 122 items are folded in after audit. The gold audit follows the Platinum method. Run Vault and a different-family strong model on every item. Send every disagreement with gold to a human. Tag each one gold-wrong, gold-incomplete, ambiguous (drop) or system-wrong, and promote items from `draft` to `verified` only after that review ([Vendrow et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03461v1)). Hamel Husain's warning applies directly here: a developer standing in for the domain expert "is a recipe for disaster", so the 10-K and contract strata need a reviewer who can read a 10-K and a contract ([hamel.dev](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/llm-judge/)).

### Sizes for target CI widths, with clustering and paired gates

**Best-evidenced strategy.** Report the SEM. Cluster standard errors on the shared unit (the source document). Make every change decision on paired per-item differences. Anthropic's statistics guidance says clustered standard errors "can be over three times as large as naive standard errors". It calls paired differences a "free" variance reduction and reports per-question score correlations of 0.3-0.7 between frontier models (VERIFIED) ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/statistical-approach-to-model-evals); [Miller, arXiv 2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640v1)).

**For Vault.** The numbers below are INFERENCE from the binomial normal approximation. Several QASPER questions share a paper and many CUAD questions share a contract, so the effective n is smaller. With 3 questions per document and an intra-document correlation of 0.3, the design effect is 1 + (3−1) × 0.3 = **1.6**. With 10 per document it is 3.7, which is why the plan caps items per document at about 3.

| Goal (95%, two-sided) | Items needed at p=0.8 | at p=0.9 | at p=0.95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ±10 points | 62 | 35 | 19 |
| ±7 points | 126 | 71 | 38 |
| ±5 points | 246 | 139 | 73 |
| ±3 points | 683 | 385 | 203 |

Multiply by the design effect for clustered items. Claims of very high accuracy need a one-sided upper bound on the error rate. **Zero errors in 300 items** bounds the error below 0.99%. Zero errors in 122 bounds it below 2.43%, and 5 errors in 300 bounds it below 3.47% (INFERENCE, exact Clopper-Pearson one-sided 95%). A "99% faithful" claim therefore needs about 300 judged claims with at most one or two failures, plus a judge whose own error rate is known.

Paired gates are where Vault's existing convergence engine already does the hard part. Their sensitivity depends on the discordant-pair rate *d*. The minimum detectable effect at 80% power is about 2.8 × √(d/n): about **16 points at n=44**, 9.8 at n=122, 6.3 at n=300 and 4.4 at n=600 when d=0.15 (INFERENCE; regenerate with `python3 -c "import math;[print(n,round(280*math.sqrt(.15/n),1)) for n in (44,122,300,600)]"`). Per-stratum reports of about 40 items carry roughly ±12-15 points. They are diagnostic, not decision-grade, unless pooled.

**Step.** Change the report generator to print, for every metric: the overall mean with a document-clustered CI; per-stratum means with CIs, labeled "diagnostic"; and for paired comparisons, the mean difference, its CI, the discordant counts and the **minimum detectable effect**, so the gate never claims sensitivity it lacks. Run each answer twice on the smoke split to estimate within-item variance before setting margins, as Anthropic recommends.

### LLM judges and calibration: narrow binary judges, cross-family, validated on human labels

**Best-evidenced strategy.** Use one narrow binary judge per failure mode, each validated against human labels with TPR/TNR and Cohen's kappa. Use a judge from a different model family, or average two families. Thakur et al. found judges with 80%+ agreement can still produce scores up to 20 points apart. The best judge reached **kappa 0.84** (GPT-4 Turbo), against a human-human kappa of 0.96, and judges showed "a bias toward leniency" (CLAIMED) ([Thakur et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12624v2)). Self-preference is measurable: models rate their own outputs higher even when humans rate them equal (CLAIMED) ([Panickssery et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13076v1)). CRAG averages a ChatGPT judge and a Llama-3-70B judge for exactly this reason, reaching **94.7% and 98.9%** F1 agreement with humans (VERIFIED) ([CRAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04744)). For completeness specifically, RAGAS factual correctness decomposes answer and reference into claims and computes precision TP/(TP+FP) and recall TP/(TP+FN) (CLAIMED, docs) ([RAGAS](https://docs.ragas.io/en/latest/concepts/metrics/available_metrics/factual_correctness/)). Husain's calibration recipe splits labels 10-20% train (few-shot examples), 40-45% dev and 40-45% frozen test, says "Below 60 examples, the confidence intervals are often too wide", and reports Honeycomb reaching more than 90% agreement in three iterations (VERIFIED) ([hamel.dev](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/llm-judge/)). ARES's prediction-powered inference corrects judge bias with about **150 or more human labels** and produced valid CIs 6.1-7.4 points wide (VERIFIED) ([ARES](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09476v2)).

**For Vault.** The completeness judge should be rewritten, not tuned. Its false fails come from comparing a prose answer to a prose gold that lists more than the question requires. The replacement asks one question per required claim ("Is required claim k entailed by the answer? yes/no"), accepts paraphrase, equivalent numeric formats and unit conversions, and does not penalize extra correct detail. Completeness becomes recall over required claims. Faithfulness should lean on what Vault already has. Because `cited_text` is code-copied, the only judged step is "does sentence E3.1 entail segment S?", which is the narrowest possible judge task. Vault's per-segment support check agrees 92% with the judge (kappa 0.83). Both are OpenAI-family models, and neither has been validated against human labels, so that agreement is consistency, not accuracy. The project already reserves the `flagship` role for judge calibration. A cross-family second judge through the dormant Anthropic adapter is the CRAG-style mitigation. It is a design change, so it needs a stated justification before it is turned on.

**Step.** Label 150-200 (question, answer) pairs per judge (completeness, segment support, abstention correctness), with at least 30-50 per class in each of dev and test. Oversample known failures so the fail class has 50-100 examples, as Eugene Yan advises ([Eugene Yan](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/product-evals/)). Double-label about 20% to measure human-human kappa. Accept a judge only when its frozen-test kappa is at least 0.75 and its TNR on truly correct answers is at least 0.9. Use PPI over the human-labeled subset for headline numbers. Re-validate whenever the generator, judge model or prompt version changes. Vault's prompt-version bump already gives that trigger.

### Observability and error attribution: a deterministic earliest-faulty-stage cascade

**Best-evidenced strategy.** Attribute each failure through claim-level entailment between gold claims, retrieved chunks and response claims (RAGChecker), backed by human-coded error analysis. RAGChecker defines claim recall, context precision, context utilization, noise sensitivity, hallucination and faithfulness from those three sets. In its meta-evaluation it correlates with humans at **Pearson 61.93**, against RAGAS 48.31 and a human ceiling of 70.09 (VERIFIED) ([RAGChecker](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08067v2)). The weak link is automated stage attribution. RAGEC's earliest-faulty-stage cascade agrees with humans on the stage only **57.8%** of the time, and on error type 40.3%. Humans blame retrieval more often than the classifier does, which blames generation more often. On its benchmark, errors split as generation 32.7%, retrieval 31.5%, chunking 29.7% and reranking 7.1% (VERIFIED) ([RAGEC](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13975v2)). The OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions are still at **Development** status in a separate repo. They define a `gen_ai.retrieval` span and a `gen_ai.evaluation.result` event whose `score.label` "SHOULD have low cardinality" (VERIFIED) ([semconv-genai](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/blob/main/docs/gen-ai/gen-ai-events.md)).

**For Vault.** Vault can replace RAGEC's weakest part, the LLM stage call, with code over its ledger. Gold evidence handles against parsed sentences tell whether the evidence was extracted and chunked intact. Gold handles against retrieved candidates give a retrieval miss. Gold present before the cutoff but absent after it gives a rerank or truncation loss. Gold retrieved but not cited gives a utilization failure. `CITATION_UNRESOLVED` and cited-not-in-gold are citation failures. Only "generation was wrong despite the right evidence" needs a judge. A further stage, "grading", records judge-versus-human disagreement, so judge errors stop being booked as system failures. Each failed item gets one reason code, which fits the reason-code-plus-template architecture.

**Step.** Add a `stage_attribution` report to the eval harness. Show counts per stage and per reason code with clustered CIs, and diff them between paired runs. Map the existing spans onto the semconv names in one module (`app/observability/genai_semconv.py`). Content attributes stay off, and reason codes go into `gen_ai.evaluation.result` labels, never free-text explanations. For online use, run deterministic checks on 100% of traffic (resolution rate, abstain rate by reason code, top-1 retrieval margin, cost and latency per node) and an async `fast`-role support judge on a 5-10% sample under a daily USD cap. Husain describes this pattern as "cheap checks on every trace and an expensive judge on a nightly sample" ([Evals FAQ](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/)). The CI gate has two tiers. Tier 0 runs on every PR at no cost: replay and retrieval-only, covering schema validity, citation resolution and recall@k. Tier 1 runs on prompt, model or routing changes: the about-150-item smoke split, paired against a pinned baseline run ID with non-inferiority margins, with injection and abstention gated separately as must-not-regress invariants.

## Retrieval misses are half the failures, and they are fixable at index time

Eight of the 16 failures are retrieval misses on QASPER papers and 10-K tables, and recall@8 is 0.73. The strongest evidence points to changes at ingest and index time. They add no query latency and cost a one-time re-index, which makes them the best first system changes.

### Parsing and chunking: structure-aware units, tables whole, overlap tested

**Best-evidenced strategy.** Cut at heading, table and caption boundaries within a 250-512-token budget. Keep each table as one unit with its caption and header row. Retrieve small and expand to the section or page. In El Bachyr et al. (2026), 25% overlap raised FinanceBench MRR from **0.529 to 0.658** and TableQuest from 0.735 to 0.833, while 50% overlap was slightly worse. **pdfplumber had the best table MRR of the parsers tested (0.807)**, and semantic chunkers did worst at 0% overlap (VERIFIED) ([arXiv 2604.12047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12047)). In Unstructured's FinanceBench study, element chunks with a prefix and table description gave the best manual Q&A accuracy (**53.19% vs 36.88%** for 256-token chunks). Aggregating all granularities raised page-retrieval accuracy to about 84% (VERIFIED, VENDOR-authored) ([arXiv 2402.05131](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05131v3)). Semantic chunking's benefits are "inconsistent and often insufficient to justify the computational cost" (CLAIMED) ([Vectara](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13070v1)).

The evidence on overlap conflicts. Chroma found 400-token chunks with no overlap scored 89.5 recall, against 85.4 for 800/400, and overlap lowered IoU (VERIFIED, VENDOR) ([Chroma](https://www.trychroma.com/research/evaluating-chunking)). El Bachyr found 25% overlap helped at 512 tokens with page-level retrieval. Both can hold: overlap helps recall when boundaries cut evidence and costs precision otherwise. For Vault, overlap is an experimental arm, not a default.

**For Vault.** Vault's ~356-token median is inside the competitive band, so size is not the problem. Boundaries and table representation are. The glyph-stream invariant rules out LLM, proposition and late chunkers, which rewrite or re-encode text. It does not rule out boundary detection from pdfplumber's font sizes, bold runs and table bboxes. For tables, the citation unit should be the row. Each row's cells, as a span of the glyph stream, become a citable sentence. The column headers and table title are attached as *index* text only, so a citation resolves to "Total revenue … 2023 … $X" rather than a whole table (INFERENCE).

**Step.** Build chunker@2 with five properties. Boundaries come from detected headings, table bboxes and figure or table captions. Each chunk stores its section path. Each table is one chunk, split by row groups only when oversized. Rows are exposed as sentence handles. Abstract and caption chunks are always eligible. Run 25% overlap as a separate paired arm on the retrieval-only eval. Verify with `make eval-extraction` that offsets remain glyph-exact.

### Indexing and embeddings: contextual index text first, embedder swap second

**Best-evidenced strategy.** Contextual retrieval. An LLM writes a 50-100-token context for each chunk, which is prepended to the text fed to both the embedding and the BM25 index. Anthropic reports top-20 retrieval failures falling from **5.7% to 3.7%** with contextual embeddings, **2.9%** with contextual BM25 added, and 1.9% with reranking on top, at about **$1.02 per million document tokens** with prompt caching (VERIFIED, VENDOR; the metric is 1 − recall@20, not answer accuracy) ([Anthropic](https://anthropic.com/research/contextual-retrieval)). Prose prefixes beat keyword lists: "Keywords Chipper" page accuracy was 46.10%, against 67.38% for prefix plus table description (VERIFIED) ([arXiv 2402.05131](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05131v3)). Generated text must be additive. Summaries that *replace* table text cut Number Match from 37.2 to **18.8** on T2-RAGBench (VERIFIED) ([T2-RAGBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12071v1)). On embedders, text-embedding-3-small trails Voyage 3 Large by about 12 points on legal MLEB (73.88 vs 85.71, CLAIMED) ([MLEB](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19365v1)). text-embedding-3-large had the best R@5 (56.1) among five embedders on financial text and table retrieval (VERIFIED) ([T2-RAGBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12071v1)).

**For Vault.** A 10-K table chunk reading "Total revenue 2023" with no company, segment or statement name is the textbook case for a prefix. Vault already has optional contextual chunk headers and a rule that prefixes are never cited, so this change is mostly switching the headers on and feeding them into the tsvector as well. The embedder swap stays within the same provider and gateway, but it is a full re-embed. It goes second, after the cheaper index-text fix has been measured.

**Step.** Generate the context per chunk with the `fast` role, with the whole document as a cached prefix. The context should name the document, entity, period, section path and, for tables, the line items and periods covered. Store it in an `index_text` column that feeds both `tsvector` and the embedding input, and never touches `original_text`. Bump the prompt version, re-index the train corpus, and accept only on a paired retrieval-only win. Then run text-embedding-3-large as its own paired arm.

### Retrieval and reranking: hybrid stays, generic cross-encoders stay off, the lexical leg gets fixed

**Best-evidenced strategy.** Hybrid lexical plus dense retrieval with no generic reranker on table-heavy or legal text, plus deeper candidate lists for the hard splits. On T2-RAGBench, hybrid BM25 was best (**Number Match 41.3**, against 37.2 for base RAG). The ms-marco MiniLM cross-encoder *dropped* it to **31.8**, and HyDE to 34.0 (VERIFIED) ([T2-RAGBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12071v1)). LegalBench-RAG found the reranker "inferior compared to not using a reranker" ([LegalBench-RAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.10343v1)). Decomposition helps only when paired with reranking: on MultiHop-RAG, Hits@10 went from 0.781 to 0.810 with decomposition alone and to **0.872** with decomposition plus reranking (VERIFIED) ([arXiv 2507.00355](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00355v1)). For papers, PaperQA2's authors credit "a deep (>10) RCS ranking list" (LLM re-scoring and summarizing of each candidate) for human-level accuracy, at roughly $1-3 per query (CLAIMED) ([Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/language-agents-achieve-superhuman-synthesis-of-scientific-knowledge)). Postgres `ts_rank` has no corpus-level IDF, unlike BM25 ([ParadeDB](https://www.paradedb.com/learn/search-in-postgresql/bm25)).

**For Vault.** Vault's own finding (no detectable gain from MiniLM at +495 ms p50) matches T2-RAGBench and LegalBench-RAG, so the reranker stays off by default. Under RRF only rank order matters, so the lexical leg's query construction (AND vs OR of lexemes, `websearch_to_tsquery` behavior) and normalization probably matter as much as the BM25 formula. That is an UNVERIFIED hypothesis, but cheap to test. The QASPER misses need a recall diagnosis first. If recall@24 is much higher than recall@8, the gold is being retrieved and lost at the cutoff, and an LLM relevance pass is the fix. If recall@24 is also low, the index is at fault, and the previous two subsections are the fix.

**Step.** Run three cheap lexical arms on the retrieval-only path: OR-of-lexemes query construction, `ts_rank_cd` with length normalization, and `setweight` on section-path and title text. Report recall@{8,16,24} per stratum. Only if the QASPER stratum shows a large gap between @8 and @24, add a `fast`-role relevance pass that returns typed scores over the top 20-30 candidates, with code keeping the top k. Gate it to the paper route. A pg_search or VectorChord BM25 extension stays deferred: it is a new dependency with vendor-only quality evidence.

### Routing and agentic search: a bounded, code-owned "locate" loop for long filings

**Best-evidenced strategy.** Route complex single-document questions over long documents to an agent with `search`, `find` and `open` tools, and leave simple questions on single-shot RAG. AgenticRAG's tools are semantic or multi-query `search` (up to 5 reformulations), `find` by pattern (at most 2 passages per pattern, about an 11K-token cap), `open` (1,800-line numbered windows) and `summarize`. Its router sends only complex queries to the agent. Multi-query cut BRIGHT tool calls from 6.79 to 4.79 at comparable recall. A keyword-only agent (pdfgrep) reached only **32.71%** on FinanceBench, so the tools need semantic search, not just grep (VERIFIED) ([AgenticRAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05538v1)). Against this, one study finds agentic RAG uses about 3.3x input tokens and 1.5x time, and that well-tuned enhanced RAG can match it (CLAIMED) ([arXiv 2601.07711](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07711v2)). On FRAMES, multi-step retrieval with planning instructions reached 0.66, against 0.52 without them (CLAIMED) ([FRAMES](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12941v3)). Long context beats RAG by about **15 points on comparison questions** (LaRA, GPT-4o 79.66 vs 46.56 at 32k) but loses on hallucination detection (VERIFIED) ([LaRA](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09977v1)).

**For Vault.** This is the only documented route past 90% on 10-Ks, and it fits Vault's rules if the model only returns typed tool decisions and code executes them. The `search` tool is Vault's hybrid retrieval scoped to one document, so every index fix above also makes the agent better. That is the reason to build the agent *after* those fixes. Every tool result is returned as sentence handles, so citations stay deterministic. The cost is real. At 7.8x tokens on the routed share, Vault's $0.006 turn becomes roughly **$0.047** (INFERENCE, linear scaling, unmeasured on Vault's models).

**Step.** Add a `locate` route chosen by code: a single named document, doc type 10-K, or a low sufficiency score after the first pass. The model emits typed actions (`search(queries[≤5])`, `find(pattern)`, `open(handle_range, window)`, `answer`), and code executes them with a step cap of about 6 and a token budget enforced in the gateway. The ledger records each step. Evaluate it only on the 10-K stratum against the single-shot route, paired, under `--max-usd`. Re-test the existing whole-document route on the same stratum at the same time, because the literature's long-context gains are concentrated on exactly these documents.

## Answer-side fixes are code-owned checks, not better prompts

The remaining failures are about 5 "incomplete" answers (at least 2 of them judge errors), 3 compare or conflict answers missing a side, and 3 false refusals from the gate, which is now off. Each has a fix in which code, not the model, enforces the outcome.

### Generation and prompting: plan required elements before writing segments

**Best-evidenced strategy.** Order the structured output reason-before-answer: evidence and plan fields first, answer fields last. Put the documents before the instructions. The "JSON hurts reasoning" result was mostly an ordering artifact. In Tam et al., "100% of GPT 3.5 Turbo JSON-mode responses placed the 'answer' key before the 'reason' key", and GSM8K accuracy fell from 76.6 to 29.87 (VERIFIED) ([Tam et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.02442v3)). With reasoning before answer and a one-shot example, structured output matched or beat free text (GSM8K 0.78 vs 0.77) (VERIFIED, from a structured-generation vendor) ([dottxt](https://blog.dottxt.ai/say-what-you-mean.html)). Anthropic advises putting long documents above the query and grounding answers in quotes first (VERIFIED, VENDOR guidance, no figure) ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/long-context-tips)). On effort, GPT-5's intelligence index scored Medium 67, Low 64 and Minimal 44 (VERIFIED, independent) ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/gpt-5-benchmarks-and-analysis)). For numbers, executing arithmetic in code beat model arithmetic by **24 points on FinQA** (40.4 → 64.5) and 19 on ConvFinQA. These are 2022-era Codex results, and reasoning models likely shrink the gap (VERIFIED) ([Program of Thoughts](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.12588)).

**For Vault.** A `required_elements` field before `segments` turns "missing one side" from a judge finding into a deterministic check. Each entry names one sub-question or entity and the handles covering it, and code verifies every element has at least one resolved handle. A typed `calculation` segment (operation from an enum such as diff, ratio, pct_change or sum, operand handles and unit) lets code compute 10-K arithmetic and explain it from reason codes. Free-form code execution would be a new sandboxed service, which is not justified.

**Step.** Bump the answer prompt and schema version with three changes: `required_elements[]` first, then `segments[]`, and a `calculation` segment type. Add one in-schema example of a two-sided comparison. Raise reasoning effort from low to medium only on the compare route, and test that as its own paired arm. Check per-model effort support first, since `gpt-6.1-sol` rejects `none`.

### Multi-document compare: per-entity quotas and a coverage gate

**Best-evidenced strategy.** Enforce coverage in code. MEBench finds models "frequently fail to locate all entity and their attributes", and "even advanced models achieve only 59% accuracy" (CLAIMED) ([MEBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18993v3)). No paper isolates per-entity quotas against global top-k, so this strategy rests on the failure mode and on the decomposition-plus-rerank gain above, not on a direct ablation.

**Step.** Have query analysis return typed `entities[]` or `documents[]` (at most 4). Run one scoped retrieval per entity in parallel, merge with a minimum quota of evidence per entity, and group the evidence block by entity. After generation, if an entity has no resolved citation, emit a coverage-gap reason code. Then either make one targeted repair call or state explicitly that the side was not found. This removes up to the 3 observed missing-side failures at near-zero extra wall time, because the branches run concurrently.

### Citations: keep the handle design, measure on the field's definitions, then prune

**Best-evidenced strategy.** Cite at sentence granularity, assign or prune citations after generation with a support threshold, and track cited length. The vendors have converged on Vault's design. Anthropic's Citations API chunks documents into sentences and extracts `cited_text` itself, so citations are "guaranteed to contain valid pointers", but it is incompatible with structured outputs (VERIFIED) ([Anthropic Citations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)). OpenAI's file-search annotations point at files, not chunks (VERIFIED) ([OpenAI file search](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-file-search)). On LongBench-Cite, sentence-level citing beat 128-token chunks by **65.4 vs 51.6 F1** on the same model. Citation training cut average cited length from 220 tokens (GPT-4o) to 85 while F1 rose to 72.0 (VERIFIED) ([LongCite](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02897v3)). Google's grounding check scores each sentence as a claim, with a default `citationThreshold` of 0.6 for "fewer but stronger citations" (VERIFIED, docs) ([Google Check Grounding](https://docs.cloud.google.com/generative-ai-app-builder/docs/check-grounding)). Cohere says post-generation citations are more precise than inline ones (CLAIMED, no numbers) ([Cohere](https://docs.cohere.com/docs/rag-citations)).

**For Vault.** Vault's citation integrity of 1.00 is table stakes, not a differentiator. Its 0.62 "citation chunk precision" is stricter than the ALCE and LongCite definitions, which score per citation and count partial support as relevant. The number cannot be compared to the field's 65-72 F1 until it is re-computed under those definitions. Since Vault already cites sentences, its precision loss probably comes from citing several handles per segment, not from unit size (INFERENCE).

**Step.** First add ALCE-style citation recall (each segment's cited set entails it) and precision (each handle is necessary or helpful), plus mean handles per segment, to the report. Then extend the support-check call to return a per-handle verdict in one typed list, and have code drop a handle when the remaining handles still entail the segment (greedy leave-one-out). Accept the change only if citation precision rises with citation recall non-inferior.

### Verification: one deterministic repair pass on the support check's verdict

**Best-evidenced strategy.** Check each sentence or segment against its evidence with a calibrated checker, then delete or repair unsupported claims rather than regenerating the answer. MiniCheck checks each sentence as a claim (max over documents) and found atomic-fact decomposition costs 2-4x with "no consistent accuracy gain". MiniCheck-FT5 (770M) scored **74.7 balanced accuracy** against GPT-4's 75.3 on LLM-AggreFact, at about **$0.24 against $107** for the test set (VERIFIED) ([MiniCheck](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10774v2)). Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B reports 77.4 under a reportedly non-commercial license (VENDOR) ([Bespoke](https://bespokelabs.ai/bespoke-minicheck)). On adversarially hard cases, every detector, GPT-4o included, sits near chance: best balanced accuracy about 58 on FaithBench (CLAIMED) ([FaithBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13210v1)). Anthropic's documented pattern is to verify each claim with a supporting quote and retract it if none is found (VERIFIED, VENDOR guidance) ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/strengthen-guardrails/reduce-hallucinations)).

**For Vault.** Vault already runs MiniCheck's recipe: segment as claim, a cheap model, max over cited evidence. What is missing is an action on the verdict and a threshold set from human labels rather than a default. Raising faithfulness from 0.84 toward the mid-90s depends mostly on that action. The FaithBench ceiling means the residual few percent is partly unmeasurable, so a "99% faithful" claim must carry the checker's measured human agreement.

**Step.** When a segment fails support, code drops it, or marks it with a `CITATION_WEAK_SUPPORT` reason code shown as "unsupported" in the UI. One rewrite call per answer at most, re-checked once, with no loops. Set the support threshold on Phase 1's human-labeled segments. Consider MiniCheck-FT5 as a local service next to the reranker only if it beats the `fast`-role check in a paired test. That would be a new service and needs a design note and a license check.

### Abstention: a scored combiner with a partial-answer state

**Best-evidenced strategy.** Combine a context-sufficiency signal with confidence in a small classifier, and set the threshold on a coverage-accuracy curve. Binary sufficiency alone over-refuses. Google's Sufficient Context study found models answer correctly **35-62%** of the time with insufficient context, through parametric knowledge, partial multi-hop inference and disambiguation. Logistic regression over sufficiency plus self-rated confidence gave **2-10% more correct answers among those answered**, and fine-tuning for "I don't know" made hallucination worse: Llama on Musique went from 26.8% to 36-39% (VERIFIED) ([Joren et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06037v2)). A one-shot sufficiency autorater reached 0.935 F1 against human labels (VERIFIED) ([same](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06037v2)).

**For Vault.** The gate's 3 false refusals in 44 (6.8%) are what a binary sufficiency classifier does. Vault also cannot set a threshold today, because it lacks enough unanswerable and false-premise items to measure correct refusals. Phase 1's 80 such items fix that.

**Step.** Add a typed `sufficiency: yes/partial/no` field to an existing call, so no new call is needed. Combine it with the top retrieval score and margin and the draft's supported-segment fraction in a logistic model fit on train only. Choose the threshold from the coverage against selective-accuracy curve under an explicit target, for example a false-refusal rate on answerable items of at most 2% subject to a hallucination ceiling on unanswerable items. Three outcomes are code-applied with templates: answer, partial (answer the covered part and name what was not found), and abstain. Accept it through the paired decision with CRAG-style scoring, so a wrong answer costs more than a refusal.

## Latency: shorten the serial chain before adding stages

**Best-evidenced strategy.** Cut or overlap calls on the critical path, stream, and keep a stable cacheable prefix. OpenAI's latency guide lists the fastest model that meets the bar, fewer output fields, stable content first for caching, fewer requests, parallel independent lookups, streaming, and computing known values in code. It gives no measured gains (VERIFIED) ([OpenAI latency](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latency-optimization)). Current OpenAI caching bills cache reads at **0.05x input on GPT-6.1 Sol and Terra**, with a 1,024-token minimum, but publishes no latency percentage (VERIFIED) ([OpenAI prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)). Speculative RAG, with parallel small-model drafts and one large-model verification, cut latency **50.83%** and raised accuracy up to 12.97% on PubHealth (VERIFIED) ([Speculative RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08223v2)). Semantic answer caching produces silent false positives, up to 27.2% at a 0.9 threshold in one test (CLAIMED) ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/articles/reducing-false-positives-retrieval-augmented-generation)). Vault forbids answer caching anyway.

**For Vault.** In the current serial chain (about 2 s query analysis, 0.6 s retrieval, 2.2 s generation, 1.6 s support check; TTFT about 3.3 s), query analysis is the largest block before the first token. Two latency budgets also need stating. Every additional stage in this plan must run in parallel or off the critical path, or be routed to a minority of queries. The locate loop must be measured separately, since no paper reports its latency on FinanceBench.

**Step.** Start hybrid retrieval on the raw query at t=0, in parallel with query analysis, and run only the extra per-entity retrievals after analysis returns. That takes roughly 0.6 s off simple queries (INFERENCE). Trim query analysis to the typed fields actually consumed, on the `fast` role at the lowest supported effort. Stream segments as they validate, with a visible "verifying" state, and run the support check per segment rather than after the whole answer. Place the static system prompt, schema and example first (at least 1,024 tokens), and record `cached_tokens` and TTFT with and without a cache hit before claiming any gain. Do not adopt HyDE (an extra pre-retrieval generation call that hurt on tables), predicted outputs (wrong model families, no structured outputs) or semantic caching.

Two sequencing choices deserve defense. The agentic loop is ranked ninth despite having the highest ceiling. Its `search` tool *is* the hybrid retriever, so ranks 1-3 raise the agent's ceiling too, and building it first would measure an agent sitting on a weak index. The evaluation rebuild is ranked zero despite fixing no system failure, because at n=44 the paired MDE of about 16 points is larger than the expected effect of every change on this list. A plausible failure budget for the 44-item train split illustrates this (INFERENCE). Correcting 2 judge errors alone moves answer_pass from 0.64 to about 0.68 without any system change. Recovering half the retrieval misses adds about 9 points. Closing the missing-side compares adds about 7. Yet each of those steps is individually undetectable at the current size. It is also unclear from the record whether the 0.64 baseline was measured with the evidence gate on. If it was, part of the 3 false refusals is already recovered, and the baseline should be re-run with the gate off before anything else is compared to it.

### What to keep, rewrite and retire

Keep the parts the evidence vindicates: pdfplumber glyph-mapped extraction (top table MRR among parsers tested), sentence handles with code-copied `cited_text` (the vendor-converged design), hybrid RRF, the gateway and role routing, the support check as an engine, the paired-test convergence engine, reason codes with templates, the run ledger and OTel spans, and the never-cache-answers rule. Rewrite five things: the completeness judge (claim-level, reference-based, cross-family, human-validated), the gold format (atomic required claims with handle-range evidence), the chunker's boundary logic, the evidence gate (into a scored combiner), and the answer schema (plan-first, calculation segments). Leave these off or retired, each backed by negative or weak evidence: the MiniLM cross-encoder by default; HyDE; semantic, proposition and late chunking; claim-level atomic decomposition inside the checker; fine-tuning for abstention; blind multi-query fusion (an industry deployment found "retrieval-level improvements do not reliably translate into end-to-end gains", CLAIMED) ([arXiv 2603.02153](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02153v1)); semantic answer caching; and predicted outputs.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the 95-99% goal. The systems that report numbers in that range do not have better retrievers. They have harnesses that change what is being measured: a 92% that is really "agent navigates one known filing", an 85% that is really "precision on answered questions", a 100% that is really "pointers are valid by construction". Vault already owns the last one. It can honestly earn the second through calibrated abstention and verify-and-repair, and it can approach the first only on 10-Ks with a routed locate loop. For contracts and papers, the credible public claim is high selective precision with transparent coverage, not a single accuracy number.

The less obvious implication is that, at Vault's current scale, the measuring instrument is the largest source of error. Label noise accounts for more than half of failures on most benchmarks, Vault's own audit found judge errors in 2 of 5 failures, and the paired detection threshold is about 16 points at n=44. Under those conditions, every system change is a guess until the ruler is fixed. That makes the human-labeled calibration set the highest-leverage artifact the team can build. It is also the one that survives a hostile reader, because it is the only number Vault cannot produce by grading itself.
